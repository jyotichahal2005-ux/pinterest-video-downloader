How to Download Pinterest Videos on iPhone (2026 Guide)

Saving a Pinterest video on an iPhone is mostly about using the right link and knowing where your browser puts the file. This guide keeps the process simple, avoids unnecessary apps, and helps you find the MP4 again later when you actually want to watch it. Everything here works in Safari, which ships with every iPhone, so there is nothing extra to install.

Step 1

Start with the pin, not a screenshot

Open the Pinterest app or Pinterest in Safari and navigate to the video pin you want to keep. Tap Share, then choose Copy link. A copied pin URL gives a downloader something useful to read; a screenshot or a copied caption does not. Public pins are the most reliable because they can be viewed without a private session. If you are on iPadOS, the same steps work, and you can often drag a link directly into the field later. Copying the clean pin URL rather than a messy search result keeps your next step fast and avoids errors.

Step 2

Use Safari and paste the full URL

Open Safari and go to PinSaver. Tap the link field, choose Paste, and confirm the address looks like a Pinterest pin link. Tap Download and wait while the pin is read. If the app copied a shortened share URL, that is fine; the service can resolve many shared links. Keep the page open while the available video files load. Safari handles all of this in the background, so you do not need a second app, a file manager, or a special Safari shortcut. If you prefer, you can also open the link in a private tab, which behaves the same way.

Quick tip If the Paste button is not showing, tap-hold the input field and choose Paste from the context menu — no clipboard permission needed.

Step 3

Choose the quality that fits your use

The result can show more than one MP4 quality. A higher resolution is useful for a larger screen, while a smaller file is easier to store or share privately. The choices depend on what the original pin exposes, so PinSaver cannot create a quality that was not present in the source. Tap the option you want and allow Safari to download it. Do not assume every pin has every quality; a recipe clip might only have one version while a longer video might offer several. Choose based on your screen, your storage, and how much you care about crisp detail.

Step 4

Find the file and keep your library tidy

On recent iPhones, downloads normally appear in the Files app under Downloads, often inside iCloud Drive or On My iPhone depending on your Safari setting. Open the file to check it, rename it with a descriptive title, and move it into a folder if it is something you will reference again. If you only need a short-lived reference, deleting it later keeps storage under control. You can also add the file to a folder on your home screen, mark it as a favorite, or include it in a shared album if you want to reach it from another device.

A note about permissions

Save videos for personal reference when you have the right to do so. Do not repost a creator's work as your own or remove context that matters. For a quick fix when a pin will not load, check that it is public and that Safari has permission to download files. You can see that in Settings → Safari → Downloads, where you control the default location and whether Safari asks before downloading. Keeping your browser updated also prevents older permissions or security rules from silently blocking a file you are trying to save.

Remember Keep the creator's handle and the pin URL with your notes. For personal reference use, that context is all you need.
